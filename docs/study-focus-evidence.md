# Study listening presets: evidence and limits

The ten Study Focus cards are task-aware search prompts, not a hand-vetted catalog or clinically validated interventions. Searches return external music results that can vary over time; the app does not inspect their contents or measure study performance. The timer is visual only and does not control playback.

The descriptions prioritize optional instrumental/no-lyrics starting points for reading and verbal revision because a systematic review found background music with lyrics tended to be more detrimental than instrumental music for memory and language-related tasks. The review found effects were often null or mixed overall, and difficult tasks were more vulnerable; it does not establish that instrumental music improves focus or that any genre is best for mathematics, physics, coding, exams, or an individual listener. Silence is a valid choice, especially when music distracts.

Source: Yiting Cheah, Hoo Keat Wong, Michael Spitzer, and Eduardo Coutinho, “Background Music and Cognitive Task Performance: A Systematic Review of Task, Music, and Population Impact,” *Music & Science* 5 (2022). DOI: [10.1177/20592043221134392](https://doi.org/10.1177/20592043221134392). The review covered 95 articles and 154 experiments and cautions that findings are inconsistent and task- and population-dependent.
